Understanding Loan Amortization & Monthly Payments

Whether you're purchasing a new home, buying a car, or looking into personal loans, calculating your estimated payment and total interest cost is a critical first step. By using an amortization calculation, you can determine how much of your payment goes towards reducing the principal vs. paying interest.

What is Principal and Interest?

Every standard loan installment is divided into two parts:

  • Principal: The original money you borrowed. Paying down the principal reduces the outstanding balance of your loan.
  • Interest: The fee charged by the lender for borrowing the money. Interest is calculated based on the current remaining balance.
At the beginning of a long-term loan (like a 30-year mortgage), the majority of your monthly payment goes toward interest. Over time, as the principal balance decreases, less interest accumulates, and a larger portion of your payment goes toward paying down the principal.

How is the Monthly Payment Calculated?

The monthly payment (P) is calculated using the following mathematical amortization formula:

P = L * [ c * (1 + c)^n ] / [ (1 + c)^n - 1 ]

Where:

  • L = Total loan amount (Principal)
  • c = Monthly interest rate (Annual rate divided by 12 months)
  • n = Total number of payments (Years multiplied by 12 months)

Strategies to Minimize Interest Costs

If you want to save money on your loan, consider the following strategies:

  1. Make Extra Principal Payments: Even adding $50 or $100 extra to your principal payment each month can shave years off your loan and save thousands in interest.
  2. Choose a Shorter Term: A 15-year mortgage will have higher monthly payments than a 30-year mortgage, but the overall interest paid will be significantly lower because the interest rate is lower and the debt is cleared twice as fast.
  3. Refinance: If interest rates fall after you take out your loan, refinancing to a lower rate can lower both your monthly payment and your total interest costs.
