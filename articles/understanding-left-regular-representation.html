The Role of Inverses in Left Regular Representation

In the study of group theory, the concept of a left regular representation of a group $G$ is pivotal to understanding how groups act on function spaces. A common expression of this action is defined as:

$( ho(g)f)(x) = f(g^{-1}x)$ for all $g,x\in G$, $f\in \mathbb{C}^G$.

The requirement of using the inverse $g^{-1}$ in this formulation ensures that the action preserves the structure of the group. If we were to omit the inverse, the mapping generated by the action would not respect the necessary homomorphic properties.

For a function $f$ and elements $g$ and $h$ from the group, we expect that:

  • $( ho(gh)f)(x) = f((gh)^{-1}x)$
  • = f(h^{-1}(g^{-1}x)) = ( ho(h)( ho(g)f))(x)$

In conclusion, if we do not utilize the inverse in the representation, the equality $(gh)x = g(hx)$ would break down, leading to loss of group structure. The properties of group homomorphisms necessitate this inversion to maintain the coherence and functionality of the left action.
