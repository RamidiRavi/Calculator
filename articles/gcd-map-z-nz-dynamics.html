Exploring the Dynamics of the GCD Map on Z/nZ

Introduction

The recent exploration of the map $C_n(x) = x + \gcd(x,n)$ defined on $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ presents exciting insights into number theory. With fixed $n \ge 1$, this functional graph not only reveals intriguing properties of GCDs but also establishes a framework for understanding the structure of orbits within modular arithmetic.

Orbit Dynamics and Length

As outlined, the map generates orbits that consistently ascend until they converge at $0$. This property allows for the meticulous counting of steps within these orbits. The function $T_n(x)$, which calculates the steps required for any initial value $x$ to reach zero, highlights the behavior of these orbits, with the maximum path length equating to $S(n)$.

Potentialities Beyond Standard Literature

Despite its fascinating dynamics, the interpretation of this GCD map appears to be somewhat obscure in existing literature. While the OEIS entry for $A059975$ acknowledges various aspects of number theory, a dynamic interpretation linked to functional graphs seems to be uncharted territory. This opens doors for further exploration and research, inviting mathematicians to delve deeper into this area of study.

Seeking Expert Validation

For undergraduate researchers venturing into this realm, establishing validity in proofs and methodologies is crucial. Engaging with experts may unveil insights and highlight potential oversights. The collaborative spirit in mathematics fosters growth, and as such, constructive feedback on proof sketches or theorems paves the way for significant advancements in understanding the GCD map and its broader implications in mathematical theory.
