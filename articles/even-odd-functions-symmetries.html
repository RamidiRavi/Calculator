Discovering Symmetries: Even and Odd Functions Explained

In mathematics, functions exhibit unique symmetries, classified broadly into even and odd functions. An even function about a point $a$ satisfies the property $f(a+t)=f(a-t)$, meaning it mirrors across the vertical line at $a$. On the other hand, an odd function about a point $b$ follows $g(b+t)=-g(b-t)$, demonstrating rotational symmetry around the point $b$.

When defining a new function $h(x)=f(x)g(x)$, it is crucial to determine under what conditions $h$ becomes odd about a point $c$. A necessary and sufficient condition for $h$ to be odd about $c$ is that the symmetry centers $a$ and $b$ must be equidistant from that point $c$. This can be mathematically expressed as $c = rac{a+b}{2}$. Furthermore, $f$ should be even, and $g$ should be odd to ensure that their product appropriately reflects the odd symmetry about $c$.

A systematic method to determine $c$ can be derived from analyzing the properties of the functions. By identifying the mid-point between the symmetry centers and ensuring that the forms of $f$ and $g$ maintain their respective symmetries, one can ascertain the attributes of $h$. For example, if $f(x)=x^2-1$ (even) and $g(x)=x-1$ (odd), the product $h(x)=(x^2-1)(x-1)$ remains odd about the point $c=0$.
