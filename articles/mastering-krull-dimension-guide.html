Simplifying the Calculation of Krull Dimension in Algebra

The Krull dimension is a crucial concept in commutative algebra, reflecting the "height" of a ring through the lens of its prime ideals. While several examples exist, let’s simplify the computation of the Krull dimension for specific rings: $R = \mathbb{C}[x, y]/(x^{100}, y^{100})$ and $R = \mathbb{F}_5[x]/(x^5 - 1)$.

Understanding the Rings

  • $R = \mathbb{C}[x, y]/(x^{100}, y^{100})$ is a quotient ring where both variables are annihilated at high powers, effectively limiting the behavior of prime ideals.
  • $R = \mathbb{F}_5[x]/(x^5 - 1)$ is an example of a finite field extension, where the polynomial simplifies the structure of the ring.

Building a Chain of Prime Ideals

To compute the Krull dimension, we seek a maximal chain of prime ideals. In both cases, the easiest approach is to identify prime ideals existing in these quotient rings. For $R = \mathbb{C}[x, y]/(x^{100}, y^{100})$, we can consider the prime ideals (x) and (y), leading us to a chain: \[(0) \subset (x) \subset (x, y).\] This chain consists of two steps, hence indicating that the dimension is 2.

Final Steps with Finite Fields

For the finite field example, examine the prime ideals associated with $R = \mathbb{F}_5[x]/(x^5 - 1)$. The polynomial \(x^5 - 1\) factors completely in \(\mathbb{F_5}\), showing that the prime ideals are generated by linear factors. Here, a chain could be formed as follows: \[(0) \subset (x - a)\] for various \(a\) in \(\mathbb{F_5}\). This establishes a dimension of 1.

Conclusion

In summary, start by understanding the structure of the ring by identifying relevant prime ideals and constructing maximal chains. The Krull dimension quantifies the depth of these chains and serves as a powerful tool in algebra.
