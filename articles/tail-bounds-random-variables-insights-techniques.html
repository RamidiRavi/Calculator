Understanding Tail Bounds and Moment Conditions in Random Variables

In the realm of probability theory and statistics, understanding the behavior of random variables is paramount, particularly when assessing their tail properties. A recent exploration into random variable \( Y \) reveals that if certain moment conditions are satisfied, specifically that \( \mathsf{E}|Y|^p \leq p^{p/2}A^{p}+p^{2p}A^2B^{p-2} \) for \( A, B > 0 \) and \( p \geq 2 \), we can derive meaningful bounds on the tails of these distributions. This result showcases an intriguing interplay between sub-Gaussian and sub-Weibull regimes, illuminating the central features of their statistical behavior.

Building on this foundation, we can derive tail bounds using \( \mathsf{P}(Y \geq u) \leq rac{\mathsf{E}|Y|^p}{u^p} \). This calculation leads to insightful inequalities that inform us about the probabilities of extreme values of \( Y \). The relationship between the parameters \( A \) and \( B \) becomes crucial, especially as we consider how the upper bounds incorporate variance and uniform constraints.

By minimizing over \( p \geq 2 \), we can subsequently extend these results to the broader case of \( p > 0 \) through Lyapunov's inequality.
