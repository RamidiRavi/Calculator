Unlocking the Intricacies of Monoids in CMon

In the realm of algebra, the structure of monoids provides a fascinating foundation for understanding mathematical relationships. The recent discussion around the beautiful analogy in CMon invites us to explore the connections between multiplication submonoids and free commutative monoids, particularly when considering the commutative properties of M and N.

Given two multiplicative submonoids M, N \leqslant \mathbb{N}^{ imes}, it is established that MN \simeq M imes N under certain conditions, like when M \cup N = \mathbb{N} and M \cap N = 1. Extending this analogy to free commutative monoids, we can induce an isomorphism M' imes N' \simeq MN by embedding them appropriately within M and N.

However, the real challenge lies in the quest to complete the analogy: how do we conceive of M' \otimes N' within the structure of \mathbb{N}^{ imes}? The suggestion of constructing an internal congruence relation to simulate the external relations formed through the tensor product offers a promising avenue for exploration. By defining congruence relations that encompass identity and bi-multiplicative properties, we could potentially find a meaningful connection between these elements.

As we navigate this intricate landscape, it is crucial to maintain an open mind about the possible constructions and mappings. The potential of internal representations in CMon could yield new insights, bridging the gap between abstract algebraic concepts and their practical applications. A thorough investigation into these relationships may not only highlight mathematical beauty but also contribute to the broader field of algebra.
