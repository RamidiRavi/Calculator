Delving into Roots and Their Sum of Squares

In the realm of quadratic equations, understanding the relationship between roots can lead to intriguing discoveries. Consider the equations $x^{2}-10x+a=0$ and $x^{2}+10x-a=0$. Here, we are tasked with finding roots that not only hold mathematical significance but also exhibit opposing signs. This requirement enriches our exploration, ultimately leading us to find the sum of squares for the newly defined equation $x^{2}+2ax-5=0$.

Start by letting $x_{1}$ be the root of the first equation, while $x_{2}$ is from the second. Given the condition $x_{1}x_{2} < 0$, we can infer that one is positive and the other negative. By applying Vieta's formulas, the relationships between the roots can illuminate the path to our goal. The sum of the squares of these roots can be approached through the identity $x_{1}^{2} + x_{2}^{2} = (x_{1} + x_{2})^{2} - 2x_{1}x_{2}$.

After some mathematical manipulation, we find that the sum of squares ties back to the variable $a$. In fact, it simplifies down to $4a^{2} + 10$, showcasing the interplay between the coefficients of the original equations and the characteristics of their roots. This realization offers a profound insight into how different equations can be interrelated, and highlights the beauty of algebra in connecting disparate concepts.
