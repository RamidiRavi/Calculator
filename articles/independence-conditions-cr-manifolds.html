Are Conditions for $C^r$ Manifolds Truly Independent?

In the realm of differential topology, the definition and properties of $C^r$ $k$-dimensional parametrized manifolds play a crucial role. According to Mitsuo Sugiura in Introduction to Analysis II, a mapping $f : D o \mathbb{R}^n$, with certain conditions such as being one-to-one, having rank $k$ for all $u \in D$, and ensuring continuity of its inverse, is classified as a $C^r$ manifold. However, a thought-provoking inquiry emerges regarding the independence of these conditions, especially when we discuss mappings like $g : D o \mathbb{R}^3$.

The assertion that a mapping $g$ with rank 1 should be classified as a curve, despite being one-to-one and continuous, raises some significant questions. In some instances, the conditions might appear to contradict each other; a one-to-one mapping that only reaches rank 1 indeed seems to confine the output to a curve rather than a surface. Herein lies a critical reflection on whether the outcomes derived from these mapping conditions are mutually exclusive or if they intersect in ways that might not be immediately apparent.

Moreover, the claim that no such $g$ can exist under these parameters challenges our understanding. Is the author mistaken in their reasoning? If we extend our reasoning confines, the potential gradients in $g'(u)$ may offer insights that can resolve this confusion. Analyzing such intricate conditions could illuminate the broader dependencies among mathematical constructs, leading us to a more nuanced view of parametrized manifolds.

To wrap up, the independence of conditions stipulated for $C^r$ parametrized manifolds warrants deeper investigation. It prompts a dialogue that could clarify or even redefine our current understanding of mappings associated with manifolds, thereby enhancing theoretical discussions and practical applications in the field of mathematics.
