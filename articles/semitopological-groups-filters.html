Decoding the Construction of Semitopological Groups

In the study of topological groups, a critical question emerges: how to build a semitopological group topology from a filter base? In Taqdir Husain's Introduction to Topological Groups, the foundational proof demonstrates the existence of such a topology, provided certain conditions on the filter base are met. The interesting part arises when we delve into the nuances of neighborhood bases formed by finite intersections and translations.

The proof outlines that if a filter base $\mathcal U$ contains neighborhoods of the identity element, the generated topology through the subbase $\mathcal S=\{xU:x\in G,\ U\in\mathcal U\}$ leads to meaningful neighborhood structures around each group element. Particularly the neighborhoods $\{y\widehat U:\widehat U\in\widehat{\mathcal U}\}$ conveniently provide a foundation for transition towards proving the continuity of group operations.

One crucial point of inquiry from readers is validating the statement that the translated neighborhoods $\{\widehat U y\}$ also yield a fundamental system at each point in the topology established by $\{xU\}$. This dilemma highlights an essential concept: the inherent symmetry in group operations and the role of the filter base in maintaining equivalencies. Although the initial proof pertains to left translations, it is valid to argue that right translations yield consistent neighborhood properties as well, thanks to the underpinnings of group theory.

Ultimately, the assurance that both neighborhood fashions—left and right—lead to consistent topological structures is vital. While it may not be explicitly outlined in the book, it rests heavily on the foundation of group actions being well-defined and their relationship with the generated topological structures. For serious students of topology, grasping this concept is paramount for further exploring the depths of semitopological groups.
