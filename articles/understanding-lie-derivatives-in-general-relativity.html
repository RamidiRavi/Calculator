Demystifying Lie Derivatives and Their Sign Conventions

Lie derivatives are fundamental in understanding how tensors change along flow lines in a manifold. The exercise of calculating the Lie derivative of a 2-contravariant tensor $F^{ij}$ can be intricate, particularly when working through definitions and conventions as outlined by Wald in his comprehensive work on General Relativity.

The essence of the Lie derivative, formulated as $\mathcal L_{\xi} F = \lim_{t o 0} rac{\phi_{-t}^* F - F}{t}$, measures how the tensor $F$ changes under the flow induced by the vector field $\xi$. Understanding the transformations $\phi_t$ and its inverse $\phi_{-t}$ can clarify many confusions, particularly regarding the pullback operations.

When working through the calculations, it is vital to ensure that consistent signs are maintained. The appearance of a negative sign in front of certain terms may arise from the definitions of $\xi^i$. To obtain the correct result, re-evaluating the directionality of the flow induced by your vector field may resolve discrepancies with the expected outcome from Wald's definitions.

Geometrically, $\xi^i$ represents the flux from the point $y^i$ to $x^i$, establishing a crucial link between these points in the manifold. Understanding $\xi^i$ as $\xi^i \mapsto -\xi^i$, as you proposed, might indeed provide the clarity needed to realign your results with established conventions.
