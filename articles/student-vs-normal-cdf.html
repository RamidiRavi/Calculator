Proving the CDF Relationship Between Student's and Normal Distributions

The cumulative distribution functions (CDFs) of the Student's t-distribution and the Normal distribution exhibit intriguing properties that can be understood through statistical principles. When analyzing positive values, it's evident that the Normal CDF is consistently greater than the Student's CDF. Conversely, for negative values, the reverse holds true. This observation has profound implications in hypothesis testing and confidence interval estimation.

Theoretical Background

The Student's t-distribution is a family of distributions that is symmetric around zero, with its shape determined by the degrees of freedom (df). As the df increases, the Student's distribution approaches the Normal distribution. This difference is most pronounced in the tails of the distributions, which leads to the observed behavioral disparity when assessing their CDFs.

Visual Verification

Visual confirmation of this relationship can be derived from plotting the two CDFs across a range of values. For positive numbers, the added weight of the tails in the Normal distribution accounts for a higher cumulative probability than that of the Student's distribution. In mathematical terms, as \(x\) approaches positive infinity, the limit of Student's CDF is always less than that of the Normal CDF, which can also be derived from their definitions.

Implications for Statistical Analysis

This relationship is crucial for practitioners in finance and many other fields. Understanding that the Normal CDF will provide a more optimistic estimate for sufficiency tests can guide better decision-making. When calculating critical values for tests and constructing confidence intervals, acknowledging these differences ensures proper inferential statistics are employed, leading to more reliable results.
