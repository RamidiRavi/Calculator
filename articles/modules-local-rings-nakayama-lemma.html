Unlocking the Connection Between Finitely Generated Modules and Local Rings

A Deeper Dive into Finite Generation

In the realm of algebra, particularly when dealing with local rings and modules, one significant result shows that if \(M\) is a finitely generated module over a local ring \(R\), then \(M \otimes_R K \cong K^{\oplus n}\). This finding emphasizes the structure of modules and their tensor products, laying a foundation for further exploration in commutative algebra.

The Role of Nakayama's Lemma

Your inquiry into Nakayama's lemma is indeed crucial for establishing the desired isomorphism. As you pointed out, we begin with \(M\) being expressed as a finitely generated \(R\)-module, where the projection morphism \(\pi: R^{\oplus n} o M\) becomes key. Upon tensoring with \(K\), we obtain a \(K\)-linear map that is surjective, thereby establishing a dimension inequality: \(\dim_K(M \otimes_R K) \leq n\).

Establishing the Isomorphism

To utilize Nakayama's lemma effectively, consider the image of \(\pi \otimes \mathrm{id}\), which reflects the elements of \(M\) that remain relevant after localizing at \(K\). Since we realize that \(M\) is generated finitely over a local ring, we can invoke Nakayama's lemma to conclude that the kernel of \(\pi\) vanishes within \(M\) when tensored with \(K\). This ultimately enables us to argue that the dimension must indeed equal \(n\), leading us to the conclusion that \(M \otimes_R K \cong K^{\oplus n}\).
