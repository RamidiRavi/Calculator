Unraveling the Mysteries of Riemann Zeta Function Analogs

The Riemann zeta function, a cornerstone of analytic number theory, continues to inspire innovative mathematical exploration. Recently, new analogs have emerged that deepen our understanding of this remarkable function. One such analog is the function $ rac{\sin(z)}{z}$, which is entire and possesses zeros located at $\{n\pi|n\in \mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}\}$. This relationship gives rise to a modified version of the Riemann zeta function, expressed as $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} rac{1}{(n\pi)^s}= rac{\zeta(s)}{\pi^s}$.

Another intriguing exploration involves the modified function $ rac{\sin(\sqrt{z})}{\sqrt{z}}$, which restricts its zeros to the positive values of $(n\pi)^2$. The series $Z(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} rac{1}{[(n\pi)^2]^s}= rac{\zeta(2s)}{\pi^{2s}}$ raises compelling questions about the properties of zeta functions, especially considering that $Z(n)$ yields rational values for natural numbers $n$.

Furthermore, intriguing properties arise when examining the function $ rac{\sin(z)-z\cos(z)}{z^3}$, where the zeros correspond to the nonzero fixed points of the tangent function. The newly defined function $Z_1(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} rac{1}{\lambda_n^{2s}}$ offers insight into both its potential extension beyond the domain of convergence and its zeros and poles. Initial results indicate that $Z_1(1)= rac{1}{10}$, prompting further investigation into its broader implications.

Lastly, the analysis of Bessel functions, specifically $J_0(z)$, leads to the construction of $Z_2(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} rac{1}{a_n^{2s}}$, further enriching the discourse surrounding zeta function analogs. A crucial question arises regarding whether functions possessing non-real zeros can consistently yield a valid analog of the Riemann zeta function. This line of inquiry could unveil uncharted territories in mathematics and deepen our understanding of these intrinsic connections.
