Unlocking the Secrets of Area Calculation through Integration

Calculating the area enclosed by two curves is a fundamental application of integration in calculus. In this article, we will explore an intriguing case involving the curves y = x² and y = 2x. Understanding how to find the area of the region defined by these functions not only sharpens your mathematical skills but also deepens your appreciation for the power of calculus in solving real-world problems.

To begin, sketch the region R that is enclosed by the two curves. The curve y = x² is a parabola that opens upward, while y = 2x is a straight line with a positive slope. These two functions intersect at points that define the boundaries of region R. To find the intersection points, set the equations equal to each other: x² = 2x. After rearranging, we find x(x - 2) = 0, giving us intersection points at x = 0 and x = 2.

Now that we have the boundaries, we can determine the exact area of region R by evaluating the definite integral of the upper function minus the lower function over the interval [0, 2]. The line y = 2x is above the parabola y = x² within this interval. Therefore, the area A can be calculated as follows:

A = ∫ from 0 to 2 (2x - x²) dx. Evaluating this integral:

  • The antiderivative of 2x - x² is x² - (x³/3).
  • Evaluating from 0 to 2 gives us [2² - (2³/3)] - [0 - 0].
  • Calculating this gives us 4 - (8/3) = 12/3 - 8/3 = 4/3.

Thus, the exact area of region R is 4/3 square units. This example serves as an excellent illustration of how integration provides a mathematical tool for comprehensively understanding and calculating complex geometric areas.
