Evaluating Limits for Sequences in Economic Modeling

The study of limits plays a crucial role in understanding economic models, particularly when analyzing growth and convergence related to functions and sequences. This article focuses on a specific case where the function is defined as f(x) = c - m/x. Given that both sequences (u_n) and (v_n) are increasing and converge to infinity, we examine the limit l = \lim_{n o\infty} {v_n\over u_n}.

Initially, we can simplify our approach by noting that as n approaches infinity, both v_n and u_n grow without bounds. Consequently, we find that the function f(x) approaches a constant value c. This characteristic is essential as it influences the relationship between the two sequences. Specifically, we establish the recurrence relation between v_{n+1} and u_{n+1} and compute the ratio of these sequences.

This leads us to the relation {v_{n+1}\over u_{n+1}} = {v_n\over u_n} {1+f(v_n)\over 1+f(u_n)}. By reducing this equation in terms of c and the limits of v_n and u_n, we come to a point where we need to evaluate the limit l to determine how the sequences relate to one another in the long term.

Ultimately, this exploration reveals that the value of l not only depends on the chosen parameters c and m but also on the initial conditions set by v_0 and u_0. By conducting a more in-depth analysis of the derived limits, one can gain significant insights essential for formulating economic predictions based on these mathematical constructs.
