Unraveling the Krull Dimension in Distributive Lattices

The study of distributive lattices is a fascinating area in both algebra and topology, particularly when one delves into the concept of Krull dimension. A distributive lattice generated in the Boolean lattice $\operatorname{Sub}[0,1]$ by rational closed intervals provides a rich ground for exploring such properties. Defined as $D$, this lattice consists of elements that include arbitrary unions of rational intervals, giving rise to a variety of interesting mathematical structures.

One of the key components in understanding the structure of $D$ is the characterization of principal maximal filters. In this case, a maximal filter is considered principal if it is generated by an atom—specifically, the rational singletons in this context. This leads to interesting implications regarding the topology induced on the set of principal maximal filters, which is essentially discrete. Each open set associated with a rational singleton uniquely identifies the filter created by that atom, establishing a direct map between the atomic structure of the lattice and its filter topology.

The exploration of prime filters adds another layer to our understanding. A prime filter is defined by the closed intervals and rational singletons from which it can be generated. It becomes evident that the intersection of these intervals does not yield an empty set. Therefore, for any prime filter $\mathfrak p$, there exists a rational number $x$ within the interval $[0,1]$, reinforcing the understanding that all maximal ideals are indeed principal.

However, a significant question arises regarding the characterization of prime filters, which directly influences the determination of the Krull dimension of $D$. Intuitively, one might conclude that since all maximal ideals are principal, the dimension should be zero. Yet, a thorough characterization of these prime filters would provide concrete backing to this conjecture. Therefore, while the initial assumption points toward a Krull dimension of zero, further investigation into prime filters' structure is necessary for a definitive conclusion.
