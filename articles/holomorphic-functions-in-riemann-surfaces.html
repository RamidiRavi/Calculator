Demystifying Holomorphic Functions on Riemann Surfaces

The study of Riemann surfaces intertwines deeply with complex analysis, particularly when it comes to holomorphic functions. A recent inquiry into the proof regarding the holomorphicity of rational functions in homogeneous coordinates on the Riemann sphere sheds light on crucial aspects of this topic. The central theorem is that for two homogeneous polynomials, \( P \) and \( Q \), which share no factors, the mapping defined as \( f: \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{C}) ightarrow \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{C}) \) given by \( [X:Y] \mapsto [P(X,Y): Q(X,Y)] \) is indeed holomorphic.

The verification of this property anchors itself in a local perspective on charts. Utilizing the charts \( \varphi_x([X:Y]) = Y/X \) and a similar one for \( y \), the approach starts at points such as \( [1:z] \). We can observe that for scenarios where \( P(1,z) eq 0 \), the local expression maps holomorphically without ambiguity. However, when encountering \( P(1,z) = 0 \), the argument hinges on the non-vanishing of \( Q(1,z) \), enabling a transition through the defined charts to maintain holomorphicity.

A pivotal concern raised involves the arbitrary nature of selecting charts to achieve holomorphic functions. It is indeed vital to acknowledge that while local expressions of a function can appear differently based on the chosen charts, the intrinsic holomorphic nature of the function exists independently of this choice. This underscores the unity of the holomorphic function across various representations—an essential characteristic that supports the conclusion that \( f \) maintains its holomorphic quality throughout.

In summary, the proof underlines an important concept: the choice of charts facilitates clarity in demonstrating holomorphic properties, but the underlying function's nature remains valid universally. This intersection of algebraic and geometric perspectives furthers the understanding of Riemann surfaces and their analytic qualities.
