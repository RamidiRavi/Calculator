Investigating the Club Filter's Normality under AD

The examination of the club filter on ω₁ under the assumptions of ZF+AD raises profound questions about the nature of ultrafilters and their properties. One pivotal query is whether this club filter, proven to be an ultrafilter in ZF+AD, retains normality if we exclude the Axiom of Choice, which is critical in typical arguments.

Under the Axiom of Determinacy (AD), we find that all sets of reals are Lebesgue measurable and possess certain desirable properties. Additionally, the club filter, defined as the collection of subsets of ω₁ that intersect every club set non-trivially, indeed turns out to be an ultrafilter. The challenge, however, lies in demonstrating that this ultrafilter is normal without relying on DC. Typically, the “catch your tail” argument, which ensures that the diagonal intersection of any collection from the filter remains static and thus belonging to the filter, heavily invokes the aspects provided by DC.

The implication is that while ZF+AD guarantees the ultrafilter status of the club filter, establishing its normality may not be straightforwardly attainable under these assumptions alone. The crux of the issue rests on how the lack of DC prevents us from drawing certain conclusions about the interactions of sets in the filter. For a definitive resolution to this inquiry, one might have to delve deeper into the properties of sets and their intersections, particularly under regimes where choice is absent.

In summary, although the club filter on ω₁ remains an ultrafilter in the framework of ZF+AD, the question of its normality requires meticulous scrutiny, and it may indeed stretch beyond the reach of the axioms we currently hold. Future exploration could either affirm its normality under ZF+AD or suggest innovative paths towards understanding filter properties in a choice-less context.
