Navigate Panera's Menu Like a Pro: 5 Dietitian-Backed Healthy Food Choices

Eating out doesn't have to derail your health and wellness goals. With a little guidance and a keen eye for nutritional details, you can make smart choices even at your favorite fast-casual restaurants like Panera. Finding the healthiest food at Panera is significantly easier when you have expert insights. Registered dietitian Joanna Gregg provides her top picks and invaluable tips, focusing on orders that deliver essential nutrients like protein, fiber, and plenty of produce, alongside practical customization strategies to help each meal align with your daily targets.

Her advice goes beyond just choosing "low-calorie" items; it’s about understanding the nutritional profile of your meal and how to adjust it to fit your individual needs, especially keeping an eye on components like sodium, which can often be surprisingly high in restaurant fare.

What a Dietitian Looks For Before Ordering

Before diving into specific menu items, it's crucial to understand the foundational principles a dietitian uses when evaluating a menu. This approach empowers you to make informed decisions not just at Panera, but at any restaurant.

Prioritizing Protein, Fiber, and Produce

“My first stop on any restaurant menu is protein, fiber, and produce. Then I check sodium to see how the meal fits into the bigger picture of a balanced day,” Gregg explains. This quick yet comprehensive menu scan simplifies what can often feel like an overwhelming list of options.

Protein: The Satiety Powerhouse

Protein is a macronutrient vital for countless bodily functions, from building and repairing tissues to producing enzymes and hormones. From a dietary perspective, protein is particularly effective at promoting satiety, the feeling of fullness and satisfaction after eating. Studies consistently show that higher protein meals can help reduce overall calorie intake by keeping hunger at bay for longer periods. (4) This means a protein-rich meal at Panera can prevent you from feeling hungry again shortly after, making it a valuable component for weight management and stable energy levels throughout your day.

Fiber: The Digestive Workhorse

Fiber is another critical nutrient often lacking in the average diet. The FDA lists the Daily Value for fiber as 28 grams per day, yet many people fall short. (2) Fiber, found abundantly in fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and legumes, plays a multifaceted role in health. It aids digestion, promotes gut health by feeding beneficial bacteria, helps regulate blood sugar levels, and can even contribute to lowering cholesterol. Like protein, fiber also adds bulk to meals, enhancing satiety without adding significant calories. When scanning a Panera menu, look for items boasting higher fiber counts to boost your daily intake.

Produce: Vitamins, Minerals, and Antioxidants

Incorporating plenty of fruits and vegetables into your meals ensures a rich supply of vitamins, minerals, and antioxidants, essential compounds that protect your cells from damage and support overall health. Gregg's emphasis on produce means looking for salads, bowls with ample vegetables, or simply adding a side of fruit to your order. These nutrient-dense choices contribute significantly to your daily micronutrient requirements.

Understanding Sodium Levels

While protein, fiber, and produce are key for nutritional value, sodium always warrants a closer look, especially when dining out. The recommended Daily Value for sodium is 2,300 milligrams per day. Anything providing 20% (460mg) or more of this value per serving is considered high sodium. (3) Many restaurant meals, even seemingly healthy ones, can contain a significant portion, if not exceed, your daily sodium limit in a single serving. High sodium intake is linked to increased blood pressure, a major risk factor for heart disease and stroke.

This doesn’t mean you need to avoid restaurants entirely. Instead, use these suggestions and Panera’s readily available nutrition guide as a helpful reference point. By understanding the sodium content of your meal, you can make conscious choices and balance your intake throughout the rest of your day. For instance, if your lunch is high in sodium, you might opt for lower-sodium meals for breakfast and dinner.

Making informed choices at Panera starts with knowing your personal dietary needs and goals. 5 Dietitian Picks at Panera

Panera Bread is known for its wide variety of soups, salads, sandwiches, and bowls. While choice is great, it can also make healthy eating seem complicated. Joanna Gregg’s top 5 picks cut through the noise, offering balanced, nutrient-dense options with practical customization advice. The nutrition numbers below come directly from Panera’s official Nutrition Guide. Keep in mind that these are based on standard menu builds, so your final order may vary slightly by location, preparation, and any customizations you make. (1)

1. Green Goddess Cobb Salad with Chicken

Nutrition Snapshot (Full Size): 580 calories, 34 grams of fat, 30 grams of carbs, 7 grams of fiber, 15 grams of sugar, 40 grams of protein, and 1,980 milligrams of sodium. (1)

Why this pick?

This salad stands out as one of Gregg’s top choices primarily due to its robust protein content. “With 40 grams of protein and 7 grams of fiber, you’re getting a lot of nutritional value that will keep you full and satisfied,” Gregg says. The combination of protein from the chicken and fiber from the leafy greens and other vegetables (like avocado and tomatoes) makes this a powerfully satiating meal. It truly feels like a full, substantial lunch or dinner, dispelling the myth that salads are always light or unsatisfying. The variety of vegetables also ensures a good intake of essential vitamins and minerals.

How to customize it:

To make this already good option even better, Gregg suggests a few simple tweaks. Asking for the salad without bacon is a smart move to reduce both sodium and total fat content, as bacon is notoriously high in both. You can also opt for a side apple instead of the standard baguette or chips for a lighter, fiber-rich addition that provides natural sweetness without extra sodium or refined carbohydrates. Another excellent strategy is to ask for the Green Goddess dressing on the side, allowing you to control the amount you use. Even a small reduction in dressing can significantly lower calories, fat, and sodium.

What to watch:

While nutritionally dense, the full-size Green Goddess Cobb Salad does come with a significant sodium count at 1,980 milligrams. This brings it very close to the FDA's recommended Daily Value of 2,300 milligrams per day in a single meal. (1, 3) If you are monitoring your sodium intake due to health concerns or simply trying to eat a lower-sodium diet, this is an important factor to consider. Gregg recommends opting for the half size to cut sodium, calories, and fat almost in half, or being very conservative with the dressing. Balancing this meal with low-sodium choices for your other meals throughout the day is also a wise strategy.

2. Sesame Ginger Chicken Market Bowl

Nutrition Snapshot (Whole Bowl): 930 calories, 44 grams of fat, 95 grams of carbs, 11 grams of fiber, 28 grams of sugar, 38 grams of protein, and 3,200 milligrams of sodium. (1)
Nutrition Snapshot (Half Bowl): 470 calories, 22 grams of fat, 47 grams of carbs, 5 grams of fiber, 14 grams of sugar, 19 grams of protein, and 1,600 milligrams of sodium. (1)

Why this pick?

Gregg highlights this bowl for its impressive combination of fresh vegetables and substantial protein, making it a genuinely well-rounded meal. “Packed with vegetables and protein, this bowl is a well-rounded, Asian-inspired twist on the power bowl concept,” she notes. The whole bowl delivers 11 grams of fiber and 38 grams of protein, which are excellent numbers for satiety and nutritional support. (1) It offers a colorful array of ingredients that contribute diverse micronutrients, creating a flavorful and satisfying experience that moves beyond typical sandwich and soup combinations.

How to customize it:

The primary recommendation for customizing the Sesame Ginger Chicken Market Bowl is to ask for the dressing on the side. This simple action gives you direct control over the amount of dressing used, which can dramatically impact the total calories, fat, and sodium. The sesame ginger dressing, while delicious, is a significant contributor to the bowl's overall nutritional profile. By adding only a portion of the dressing, you can enjoy the vibrant flavors while making it a lighter option. Consider starting with half the dressing and adding more only if truly needed.

What to watch:

Similar to the Cobb salad, the whole Sesame Ginger Chicken Market Bowl is very high in sodium, clocking in at 3,200 milligrams. This far exceeds the FDA's Daily Value for sodium in a single serving. (1, 3) For individuals managing blood pressure or simply aiming for a lower-sodium diet, the whole bowl might be too much. The half bowl, with 1,600 milligrams of sodium, is often a more manageable choice while still providing 19 grams of protein and 5 grams of fiber. Additionally, the whole bowl's calorie count of 930 might be higher than desired for some, depending on their daily caloric goals. Choosing the half portion or sharing the full bowl are practical solutions.

3. You Pick 2: Cup of Vegetarian Black Bean Soup + Half Strawberry Poppyseed Chicken Salad

Combined Nutrition Snapshot: 300 calories, 8 grams of fat, 50 grams of carbs, 16 grams of fiber, 16 grams of sugar, 18 grams of protein, and 1,490 milligrams of sodium. (1)

Why this pick?

This "You Pick 2" combination is Gregg’s recommendation for a lighter yet incredibly nutrient-dense option. The cup of Vegetarian Black Bean Soup alone contributes 90 calories, an impressive 12 grams of fiber, and 5 grams of protein. Paired with the half Strawberry Poppyseed Chicken Salad, which adds 210 calories, 4 grams of fiber, and 13 grams of protein, the total meal becomes a fiber powerhouse with 16 grams of fiber and 18 grams of protein. (1) This pairing provides a diverse range of nutrients, including fiber from the beans, fresh fruit and vegetables from the salad, and lean protein from the chicken. It’s a great example of how combining two smaller items can create a perfectly balanced and satisfying meal.

How to customize it:

The "You Pick 2" concept offers inherent customization, and Gregg emphasizes making smart choices for your side. The apple is consistently the lighter, healthier pick, adding valuable fiber and natural sweetness without any additional sodium or refined carbohydrates. If you prefer something heartier and are less concerned about extra carbs, the baguette can work, but be mindful of its impact on the overall sodium and calorie count. For the salad, consider asking for dressing on the side to manage fat and sugar, and be aware that the Strawberry Poppyseed Chicken Salad is seasonal.

What to watch:

Even though this pairing is lighter in calories than many full-size entrées, the sodium can still add up. Together, the soup and salad total 1,490 milligrams of sodium, which is still a significant portion of the daily recommended intake. (1) It’s essential to be aware of this, especially if you have other sodium-rich foods planned for the day. Additionally, the Strawberry Poppyseed Chicken Salad is a seasonal item, so its availability may vary. Always check Panera’s current menu before planning your meal around it.

4. Greek Salad Stuffer with Chicken

Nutrition Snapshot: 770 calories, 51 grams of fat, 53 grams of carbs, 4 grams of fiber, 6 grams of sugar, 23 grams of protein, and 1,960 milligrams of sodium. (1)

Why this pick?

This pick is all about practicality and convenience without sacrificing flavor or significant nutrition. “Sometimes you have to eat on the go, and a soup or salad just isn’t practical,” Gregg acknowledges. The Salad Stuffer format offers the essence of a salad – fresh ingredients and protein – encased in a portable wrap-like form. This makes it an ideal choice for busy individuals between errands, meetings, or while traveling. It provides 23 grams of protein, helping to keep you full, and a good balance of flavors reminiscent of a traditional Greek salad.

How to customize it:

Given the "as written" nutritionals, customization is key here. Gregg strongly advises asking for light dressing or requesting the dressing on the side. This is crucial for managing the significant fat content (51 grams). The dressing is a major contributor to both fat and calories. Furthermore, you can significantly reduce both sodium and fat by skipping the feta cheese and olives, which are often high in both. For instance, removing olives and cheese can cut hundreds of milligrams of sodium and several grams of fat without completely diminishing the Greek-inspired flavor profile. Consider adding extra fresh vegetables if available to boost fiber without increasing unhealthy components.

What to watch:

As prepared, this stuffer is relatively high in both fat (51 grams) and sodium (1,960 milligrams). (1) This doesn't mean it's off-limits, but it absolutely underscores the importance of customization. Without adjustments, it can quickly consume a large portion of your daily fat and sodium budget. Be proactive in your order: explicitly ask for modifications like "light dressing," "dressing on the side," or "no cheese and olives." This allows you to enjoy the convenience and flavors of the Greek Salad Stuffer in a way that better aligns with your personal dietary goals.

5. Garden Avo & Egg White on Multigrain Bagel Flat

Nutrition Snapshot: 350 calories, 14 grams of fat, 40 grams of carbs, 2 grams of fiber, 4 grams of sugar, 18 grams of protein, and 680 milligrams of sodium. (1)

Why this pick?

For those looking for a healthier breakfast option at Panera, this is Gregg's top recommendation. It's notably lower in calories compared to many other Panera breakfast sandwiches, yet it still delivers a solid 18 grams of protein. (1) The use of egg whites helps keep the saturated fat content lower than a whole egg sandwich, while the inclusion of avocado provides beneficial monounsaturated fats. These healthy fats, combined with protein, contribute to greater satiety, helping you feel fuller for longer and preventing mid-morning energy crashes. The multigrain bagel flat also offers more fiber than a plain bagel, contributing to digestive health.

How to customize it:

Gregg suggests omitting the cheese for a simpler, lighter option. Removing the cheese will further reduce the calorie, fat, and sodium content, making it an even healthier choice. If you're particularly mindful of sodium, you could also inquire about asking for no added salt, though this might not always be feasible with pre-prepared ingredients. Consider adding a slice of tomato or spinach if available to boost vegetable intake. For those who want to minimize refined carbs, you could even ask for the egg and avocado served in a bowl instead of on the bagel flat, making it a lower-carb breakfast.

What to watch:

As a breakfast item, its availability is tied to your local Panera's breakfast hours, so always check before you go. While the sandwich is a good source of protein and healthy fats, its fiber content is modest at 2 grams. (1) If you're aiming for higher fiber intake, consider pairing this breakfast with a side of fruit (like a banana or apple) or ensure your lunch and dinner choices are particularly rich in fiber to compensate. Balancing your daily fiber intake across all meals is a practical approach to meeting the recommended 28 grams per day.

Simple Ways To Make Your Panera Order Work Better

Beyond choosing specific items, mastering a few simple customization strategies can transform nearly any Panera order into a healthier, more balanced meal. These small changes can significantly impact your total calorie, fat, and sodium intake without sacrificing flavor or satisfaction.

Choose Fruit When Your Main Is Salty

Restaurant meals are often higher in sodium than home-cooked ones. A simple yet effective strategy to balance a salty main dish is to opt for a fruit side. Fruit provides natural sweetness, a welcome contrast to savory flavors, and importantly, adds volume and fiber without contributing additional sodium. For example, a side apple has 80 calories, 5 grams of fiber, and 0 milligrams of sodium, while a banana offers 90 calories, 3 grams of fiber, and 0 milligrams of sodium. (1) This swap helps dilute the overall sodium content of your meal, adds crucial vitamins and antioxidants, and keeps you feeling full longer thanks to the fiber.

Put Dressing or Sauce on the Side

This is arguably one of the easiest and most impactful Panera customizations. Dressings and sauces, while delicious, are often calorie-dense and can be significant sources of fat, sugar, and sodium. By asking for your dressing or sauce on the side, you gain complete control over how much you use. For salads, instead of drenching your greens, you can dip your fork in the dressing before picking up your lettuce, ensuring every bite gets flavor without excess. This can shave off hundreds of calories and grams of fat, particularly in salads, bowls, and stuffers where large quantities of dressing are often automatically added.

Use Half Portions When Available

Panera offers many items in half portions (half salads, half bowls) or smaller cup sizes for soups. Utilizing these options can be a game-changer for portion control and managing overall intake. This is especially helpful with higher-calorie and higher-sodium items. For instance, the half Sesame Ginger Chicken Market Bowl contains 1,600 milligrams of sodium, significantly less than the whole bowl's 3,200 milligrams. (1) Half portions allow you to enjoy the flavors you love without consuming an excessive amount of calories or nutrients that might push you over your daily limits. They can also be a great way to try a variety of items without overeating.

Think About the Full Combo

The "You Pick 2" option is a popular choice at Panera, offering variety and the perception of a lighter meal. However, sodium and calorie counts can climb quickly, especially when you pair items like soup, bread, cheese, dressing-heavy salads, or salty sandwiches. Before ordering a combo, take a quick glance at the nutrition information for *both* items you plan to combine. A half sandwich and a cup of soup can easily add up to more calories, fat, and sodium than a single full entrée. MyFitnessPal, or simply Panera's nutrition guide, can help you compare your usual Panera meals and notice which swaps make the biggest difference in your overall nutritional intake.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What is the healthiest thing to eat at Panera?

There isn’t one single "healthiest" order at Panera, as the best choice depends on your individual dietary goals, preferences, and what sounds satisfying to you. However, dietitian Joanna Gregg’s top picks are excellent starting points for nutrient-dense options. These include the Green Goddess Cobb Salad with Chicken, the Sesame Ginger Chicken Market Bowl, a You Pick 2 with Vegetarian Black Bean Soup and half Strawberry Poppyseed Chicken Salad, the Greek Salad Stuffer with Chicken, and the Garden Avo & Egg White on Multigrain Bagel Flat for breakfast. Each offers a good balance of protein, fiber, and produce with customization potential.

How can I make my Panera meal more filling and satisfying?

To enhance satiety and fullness, focus on maximizing protein and fiber. Choose meals with lean protein sources like chicken, turkey, or beans. Opt for dishes rich in vegetables and whole grains, which provide essential fiber. For instance, adding an extra side of fruit or vegetables, choosing whole-grain bread options, or selecting a larger protein portion can significantly increase the satisfying power of your meal. Including healthy fats, like the avocado in the breakfast sandwich, also contributes to sustained fullness.

Are Panera's salads always healthy?

While salads often carry a "healthy" halo, it's important to look beyond just the greens. Many restaurant salads, including some at Panera, can be high in calories, fat, sugar, and sodium due to generous portions of cheese, candied nuts, crispy toppings, and heavy dressings. To ensure your Panera salad is a healthy choice, opt for lean protein, plenty of fresh vegetables, and ask for dressing on the side. Consider skipping high-calorie additions like bacon or fried ingredients, and choose lighter vinaigrettes over creamy dressings.

What are the best low-carb or keto-friendly options at Panera?

For those following a low-carb or keto diet, Panera offers several customizable options. Salads without croutons and with dressing on the side are a good start; the Green Goddess Cobb Salad with Chicken, customized to remove bacon and served with dressing on the side, is a great option. You can also order bowls and request no grains or rice. For sandwiches, ask for the fillings (like chicken, avocado, and egg whites) to be served in a bowl without the bread. Focus on grilled chicken, egg whites, avocado, and plenty of non-starchy vegetables while being mindful of dressings which can sometimes contain hidden sugars.

How can I reduce calories in my Panera order?

Reducing calories at Panera is straightforward with a few key strategies: choose half portions when available, ask for dressings and sauces on the side to control the amount you use, and opt for lean protein sources. Skip high-calorie additions like cheese, bacon, fried components, and sugary beverages. Choose fruit or vegetable sides instead of chips, baguette, or pastries. Simple swaps, like a cup of soup instead of a bowl, or a small coffee instead of a blended specialty drink, can also make a significant difference.

What are some good vegetarian or vegan options at Panera?

Panera has several excellent options for vegetarian and vegan diets. The Vegetarian Black Bean Soup is a flavorful, high-fiber choice. For salads, many can be customized by removing meat and cheese, or you can opt for the Greek Salad without chicken and feta. The Mediterranean Veggie Sandwich can be made vegan by omitting the feta cheese. Look for bowls with plant-based protein sources like chickpeas or black beans, and always double-check ingredients for hidden animal products or dairy, especially in dressings and bread.

How accurate is Panera's nutrition information?

Panera provides comprehensive nutrition information, which is generally quite accurate and a reliable guide for making informed choices. They are transparent about their ingredients and nutritional breakdowns. However, keep in mind that slight variations can occur due to differences in portioning by individual staff members, regional ingredient sourcing, and preparation methods. Use their guide as a strong reference point, but understand that exact numbers might fluctuate slightly with each order. Customizing your order (e.g., light dressing) gives you more control over the final nutritional profile.

Can I eat at Panera regularly and still maintain my health goals?

Absolutely! Eating at Panera regularly can certainly fit into a healthy lifestyle, provided you make conscious and informed choices. By focusing on Gregg's principles of prioritizing protein, fiber, and produce, being mindful of sodium, and utilizing customization options, you can create balanced and nutritious meals. The key is to view your Panera order within the context of your entire day's food intake. Balance higher-calorie or higher-sodium Panera meals with lighter, nutrient-dense options at other times to meet your overall health and wellness objectives.

Bottom Line

Panera offers a diverse menu with plenty of options that can fit into a balanced and healthy eating plan. The secret lies in knowing what to look for and how to customize your order effectively. Dietitian Joanna Gregg's picks highlight meals that are rich in essential nutrients like protein, fiber, and fresh produce, along with simple yet powerful ways to tailor your order to your specific dietary needs and health goals.

Ultimately, the "best" order is the one that not only tastes good but also aligns with your personal health objectives and seamlessly integrates into the rest of your daily nutritional intake. By applying these expert insights, you can enjoy your Panera favorites while staying on track with your wellness journey. And when you need fresh ideas or motivation, don't forget the MyFitnessPal Community can be a helpful place to see how other members build real-life meals around real-life menus.
