Exploring Connections on Differentiable Manifolds

In the realm of differential geometry, the fascinating concept of differentiable manifolds opens the door to numerous inquiries. One particularly engaging question arises: Is it possible to select a chart from the maximal atlas of a differentiable manifold that allows two points to be connected directly by a straight line? This question inevitably leads us to consider the implications of manifold structure, connectivity, and the properties of charts.

A differentiable manifold is, at its core, a space that resembles Euclidean space in small regions. Each chart acts as a homeomorphism mapping an open subset of this manifold into \(\mathbb{R}^n\). However, it’s important to note that these mappings need not exhibit convex properties. Therefore, the challenge lies in identifying a chart from the maximal atlas—that collection of charts that includes all possible compatible charts, ensuring manifold continuity—where two distinct points can be bridged by a straight line.

Assuming we are operating under the premise of a connected manifold, the exploration becomes nuanced. Connectedness implies that any two points within the manifold can be part of the same path component. However, the existence of a straight line in a chart that connects them is not guaranteed. The local nature of charts means that they can be distorted in ways that prevent a direct linear path between points, despite their connectivity throughout the manifold.

In conclusion, while the question seems straightforward, the complexities of manifold topology suggest that there is not a universal answer. Each manifold's unique properties and configuration might yield situations where a straight line cannot be drawn in any chart from the maximal atlas, presenting a rich field for ongoing research and exploration in differential geometry.
