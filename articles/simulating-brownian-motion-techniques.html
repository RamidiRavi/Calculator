Mastering Numerical Simulation of Brownian Motion

The simulation of Brownian motion, particularly the residence time in small intervals, is a critical area in mathematical finance and physics. Understanding how to accurately simulate this can greatly enhance predictive models and theoretical predictions.

To achieve a more accurate numerical method for simulating residence time in the interval $[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]$, one effective approach is employing stochastic numerical integration techniques. High-order schemes such as the Milstein method or the Euler-Maruyama method offer better precision than simple grid-point approximations by accounting for the stochastic nature of Brownian paths. Additionally, implementing adaptive time-stepping techniques can help balance precision and computational efficiency.

Regarding the scaling requirement $\Delta t \ll rac{\varepsilon^2}{D}$, this condition ensures that the discretization captures the fluctuations of Brownian motion adequately. Meeting this requirement is crucial for obtaining reliable estimates of residence time, as it minimizes the risk of missing the particle's entry and exit points in the very small interval.

Furthermore, addressing how to estimate time spent in $[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]$ based on known Brownian bridge corrections can provide valuable insight. These corrections can effectively incorporate the conditional probabilities of the path between grid points, offering a more nuanced estimate. Lastly, for comparing simulated local time $L_\varepsilon(t)$ with theoretical local time $\ell(t;X)$, one should carefully handle the limits as $\Delta t o0$ and $\varepsilon o0$, utilizing strong convergence results to validate the consistency of simulations with established theoretical frameworks.
