Unlocking the Mysteries of Primes with Scalar Product Projections

Introduction to an Innovative Theory

Recent developments in number theory have reignited curiosity about the relationships between natural numbers and prime sums. Ezadiin Redwaan's preprint introduces a groundbreaking approach by utilizing scalar product projections to examine the enigmatic connections between these two mathematical realms. At the heart of the argument lies a global boundary identity for an infinite-dimensional vector space, formulated in relation to prime numbers.

The Framework of the Proposal

In this innovative framework, Redwaan constructs vectors based on sequences of prime components and natural numbers. The critical aspect involves the regularized sum of natural numbers evaluated through the lens of the Riemann zeta function, yielding a central formula representative of the boundary conditions. By integrating concepts from geometric phase angles, the theory seeks to unite distinct analytic structures pertaining to natural numbers and primes.

Challenging Traditional Number Theory

This fresh perspective invites scrutiny of long-standing conjectures, including Goldbach’s and Polignac's. The theory posits that if certain numerical partitions cannot be achieved, it results in a striking topological contradiction, a notion that challenges conventional understandings. Moreover, questions arise regarding the potential of this approach to reconcile the disparities in analytic continuations between distinct functions associated with primes.

Implications and Future Considerations

As researchers delve deeper into the implications of Redwaan's theories, several questions demand answers. Can a meaningful link be forged between the regularized sums of numbers and primes through a coherent mathematical framework? The implications could pave the way for advancing our foundational understanding of prime distributions and the structure of numbers at large. This exploration not only encapsulates the vibrant nature of mathematical inquiry but also challenges us to rethink the constraints of established number theory.
