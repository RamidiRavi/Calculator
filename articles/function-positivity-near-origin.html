Proving Function Positivity Near the Origin: A Step-by-Step Guide

In evaluating the function $g(x, y) = 2x(3x + y^3) - 2y(-2y + x^2)$, the goal is to demonstrate its positivity within a punctured neighborhood around the origin $(0, 0)$. At first glance, the intuitive assumption is that near the origin, lower degree terms will dominate, leading to a positive outcome based on the behavior of $g(x, y)$.

To establish this rigorously, we can start by simplifying the expression. The terms can be rewritten as follows: $g(x, y) = 6x^2 + 4y^2 + O(xy^3) + O(yx^2)$, focusing on the dominant terms that clearly show a positive contribution as $|x|, |y| < 1$. Here, $6x^2 + 4y^2$ is known to be positive whenever both $x$ and $y$ do not equal zero.

Moreover, the additional terms represented by $O(xy^3)$ and $O(yx^2)$ contribute less significantly than the dominant terms when approaching the origin. Although they could vary in behavior, their presence will not outweigh the positive contribution from $6x^2 + 4y^2$. Therefore, as we limit our evaluation to a punctured neighborhood of the origin, we can confidently assert that $g(x, y)$ will remain positive.

This conclusion confirms that rigorous analysis, along with an understanding of polynomial dominance near critical points, is fundamental in assessing the behavior of functions in mathematical contexts.
