Unlocking the Secrets of Area Calculation through Integration

In this article, we delve into an essential aspect of calculus: applications of integration, particularly in calculating area and volume. The concept of integration allows us to find the area of regions defined by curves, which is invaluable in various scientific and mathematical fields.

Understanding the Region Enclosed by Curves

To illustrate the application of integration, we focus on the region R enclosed by the curves y = x2 and y = 2x. These two equations represent a parabola and a straight line, respectively, and visualizing their intersection helps us to determine the area they enclose.

Sketching the Region and Finding Points of Intersection

To find the enclosed area, we first need to determine the points of intersection of the two curves. Setting the equations equal to each other: x2 = 2x, leads us to factor the equation as x(x - 2) = 0, giving us critical points at x = 0 and x = 2. The next step involves sketching the curves to visualize region R, which is located between these intersection points.

Calculating the Area Using Definite Integrals

With the bounded region identified, we can now compute the area by evaluating a definite integral. The area A can be calculated using the integral of the upper curve minus the lower curve:

  • A = ∫[0 to 2] (2x - x2) dx

Evaluating this integral leads us to the final area of the region R, showcasing the power and utility of integration in practical applications such as geometry, physics, and engineering.
