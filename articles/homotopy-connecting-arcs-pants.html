Delving into the Homotopy of Connecting Arcs Among Boundary Components

In the fascinating realm of topology, the concept of homotopy provides profound insights into how curves can be transformed into each other within a given space. This becomes especially evident in the study of pairs of pants, a common model in geometric topology, as described in Chapter 10.6.1 of 'A Primer on Mapping Class Groups' by Benson Farb and Dan Margalit.

Consider a pair of pants $P$ with three boundary components labeled $\gamma_1$, $\gamma_2$, and $\gamma_3$. When we introduce a simple arc $eta$ connecting $\gamma_1$ and $\gamma_2$, and an orthogonal geodesic $\delta$ between these two boundaries, we can assess the homotopic properties of these arcs.

The statement that $eta$ is homotopic relative to its endpoints to a curve $eta'$, which agrees with $\delta$ outside of disjoint neighborhoods $N_1$ and $N_2$ surrounding the boundary components, is crucial. To prove this claim, one must first acknowledge the fundamental group $\pi_1(P)$ generated by $\gamma_1$ and $\gamma_2$.

Since any loop around the curves must contract back through these components, one can use the nature of simple arcs and geodesics to show that $eta$ cannot first circle around $\gamma_2$ and then $\gamma_1$. The path's properties dictate it must adhere to the geometric boundaries formed by these components. Thus, harnessing the power of homotopy, we can demonstrate this transformation's feasibility and elucidate connections between curves in this topological structure.
