The Intricacies of Unstable Equilibria in Dynamical Systems

In the realm of dynamical systems, the stability of equilibria plays a crucial role in understanding the behavior of various systems. An equilibrium point is deemed Lyapunov stable if trajectories starting close to it remain close over time. However, the scenario under discussion presents a fascinating case of instability when certain conditions are met.

Consider a vector field $F$ and a function $h$ defined on an open set. The specific conditions state that at the equilibrium point $x_0 = 0$, $h(0) = 0$ and that the directional derivative of $h$ with respect to the vector field $F$ is positive for points away from the equilibrium. Thus, as trajectories approach $x_0$, the function $h(x)$ is guaranteed to increase.

This increasing nature suggests that trajectories originating from nearby points continually move away from the equilibrium point, thereby leading us to surmise the instability of $x_0$. A sequence of points $x_n$ converging to $x_0$ with $h(x_n) > 0$ reinforces this illustration, indicating that despite being arbitrarily close to the equilibrium, these points do not return to it. If trajectories from these points remain in the vicinity of $B_\varepsilon(0)$, the increasing function implies that they ultimately escape the ball, thus confirming the lack of stability.

Therefore, the conclusion is robust: when a function like $h(x)$ increases along trajectories away from an equilibrium $x_0$ and adheres to the stipulated conditions, this result proves that the equilibrium is indeed unstable. Understanding these dynamics not only enhances our theoretical understanding but also has substantial implications in various fields like physics, biology, and engineering.
