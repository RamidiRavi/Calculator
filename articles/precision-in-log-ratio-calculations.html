Achieve High Numerical Accuracy in Log Ratio Calculation

The precision of numerical calculations is crucial, especially when dealing with small differences between values. When tasked with calculating the log ratio between two nearly identical numbers, such as a = 256.0001 and b = 256.0, traditional methods can introduce significant precision loss. Let’s delve into the three existing methods and propose a more accurate alternative.

Method Analysis

The first method, calculating ln(a / b), is straightforward but incurs substantial precision loss due to the division step. Specifically, when dividing numbers close in value, you fall prey to precision deterioration at the digits you care about the most: the trailing digits after the leading '1'.

Next, we consider ln(a) - ln(b). This method is plagued by catastrophic cancellation because the logarithmic values of a and b are nearly identical, leading to a drastic loss in precision. The difference can end up having far fewer significant digits, undermining the reliability of the result.

A Potential Solution

The third method, ln1p((a - b) / b), offers a more robust approach thanks to its reliance on ln1p(x) = ln(1 + x). This technique alleviates issues of catastrophic cancellation, as the subtraction (a - b) does not suffer from the same precision loss. While there may be some concern about the asymmetry of dividing by b but not a, in practice, this method yields results with greater fidelity.

However, if absolute precision is paramount, consider implementing a dedicated function like ln_ratio(x, y) that employs low-level machine techniques. By leveraging assembly instructions, one can achieve remarkable precision in floating-point arithmetic, tailoring the approach to maximize accuracy for specific values being computed.
