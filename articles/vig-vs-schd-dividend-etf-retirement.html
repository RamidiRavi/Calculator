Choosing Between VIG and SCHD: A Dividend Dilemma

When it comes to building a retirement portfolio, particularly one focused on dividend income, the choice between Vanguard Dividend Appreciation ETF (NYSEARCA:VIG) and Schwab U.S. Dividend Equity ETF (NYSEARCA:SCHD) presents a significant decision. With $500,000 earmarked for dividend equities, retirees find themselves at a crossroads: do they prioritize immediate income or long-term growth?

Diving into the Distinctions

The VIG is positioned as a quality-focused fund, tracking companies that have increased their dividends for over a decade. With a current yield of approximately 1.7% to 1.8%, it provides a relatively modest income, amounting to about $8,500 to $9,000 annually on a $500,000 investment. However, it boasts an expense ratio of just 4 basis points, making it an efficient hedge against inflation through compounding growth.

In contrast, SCHD focuses on more mature, value-oriented companies, yielding around 3.9%—nearly double that of VIG, translating to an annual income of around $19,500. While the higher yield is enticing for retirees seeking immediate cash flow, SCHD’s portfolio, concentrated in specific sectors, carries its own risks, particularly related to industry volatility and economic downturns.

Evaluating Performance

Historically, VIG has demonstrated stronger long-term returns, particularly during tech-led market rallies, showing returns of 56% over three years compared to SCHD's 51%. However, over the last year, SCHD's solid 26% gain has outpaced VIG's 18%, suggesting a shift back to value stocks. For retirees focusing on sustaining income in a fluctuating market, these performance metrics may influence their decision significantly.

Which ETF Fits Your Needs?

The ultimate choice boils down to individual circumstances. Investors who prioritize cash flow might lean towards SCHD, whereas those with a longer horizon, seeking quality and growth, may find VIG aligns more closely with their investment philosophy. A balanced allocation, perhaps a 50/50 split, could provide an optimal middle ground, simplifying the trade-off between current income and long-term financial health.
