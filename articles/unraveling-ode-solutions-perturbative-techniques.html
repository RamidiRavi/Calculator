Deriving Exact Solutions to ODEs Through Perturbation Techniques

In the fascinating world of ordinary differential equations (ODEs), perturbation techniques offer a pathway to uncovering solutions that might initially seem elusive. Recently, a noteworthy example is the exploration of the ODE given by v'(t) = 1 - v(t)^2; \quad v(0) = 0. While the exact solution is simply v(t) = anh(t), inserting a small parameter \varepsilon allows us to investigate a perturbative approach that yields insightful results.

By reformulating the problem with this artificial parameter, one can build a series solution by assuming v(t) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \varepsilon^n v_n(t). This generates linear ODEs order-by-order. At each stage, the coefficients can be derived recursively, leading to a sequence that intriguingly aligns with the Taylor series of the hyperbolic tangent function. The process reveals both the power of perturbative methods and their connection to deeper mathematical structures.

One fascinating aspect of this approach is the observation that the perturbative series diverges factorially at higher orders, suggesting limitations in its straightforward application. Nonetheless, employing Padé approximants instead of conventional truncation techniques offers a pragmatic way to enhance accuracy. These rational functions can capture essential features even beyond convergence, thereby providing an excellent approximation to anh(t) across an expanded domain.

To answer the questions posed: First, one can show that the recurrence relation for the coefficients a_n approximates the known Taylor series for anh(t) by performing a formal series expansion and matching coefficients. Second, methods like Borel summation could indeed provide an alternative pathway, often yielding more convergent results and numerical stability to recapture the ODE solution with impressive efficiency.
