Understanding Objects in $\infty$-Groupoids and Higher Categories

The study of higher category theory has brought about intriguing concepts that transform our understanding of mathematical structures. In particular, the relationship between $\infty$-categories and their properties, as explored in the work of Cesnavicius and Scholze, raises profound questions about objects in these categories, especially in the context of $\infty$-groupoids.

Firstly, let's consider the notion that objects in the $\infty$-category of spaces, as defined by Lurie, lack an underlying set of points. This stems from the fact that in a homotopical context, spaces are not strictly point-set structures; instead, they are homotopy types. This means that the objects behave more like collections of paths and ‘higher-dimensional analogs’ than traditional sets. Thus, they capture not only the existence of points but also the relationships and transformations that can occur between them.

Addressing the second question, it is essential to clarify that while an $\infty$-groupoid can indeed be modeled as a homotopy type, it is misleading to think of it as having a direct set of points. An $\infty$-groupoid is effectively a higher-dimensional generalization of groups where the morphisms (or arrows) connect objects in multiple dimensions. Although one may extract a notion of points from an $\infty$-groupoid in the form of isomorphism classes or connected components, these do not encompass the full richness of its homotopical structure.

In essence, a collection of objects within an $\infty$-groupoid can be understood as a homotopical framework where the focus shifts from points to the myriad relations and transformations among those points. In practical terms, this perspective allows for a deeper exploration of mathematical phenomena that transcend traditional set-based frameworks, emphasizing the significance of morphisms and transformations.
