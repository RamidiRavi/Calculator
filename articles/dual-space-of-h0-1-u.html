Unraveling the Dual Space of Sobolev Spaces

In the context of functional analysis, the question regarding the dual space of \( H_0^1(U) \) is a nuanced topic. It is correct that \( H_0^1(U) \) is a Hilbert space, and by the Riesz representation theorem, every bounded linear functional can be represented by an element in the space. This might lead to the initial assumption that the dual space \( H^* \) of \( H_0^1(U) \) is indeed \( H_0^1(U) \) itself. However, this assumption lacks a crucial distinction concerning the nature of the dual space.

The dual space of \( H_0^1(U) \) is actually \( H^{-1}(U) \), a larger space that encompasses distributions acting on \( H_0^1(U) \). To elaborate, while bounded linear functionals on \( H_0^1(U) \) can be represented in \( H_0^1(U) \), they often extend beyond the traditional \( L^2 \) inner product definitions. This connection is essential as \( F: H_0^1(U) ightarrow \mathbb{R} \) being continuous does not restrict the results to mere elements of \( H_0^1(U) \), but rather acknowledges distributions that map to functional evaluations.

To clarify, \( H^{-1}(U) \) consists of distributions, which can include functionals that are not necessarily represented in \( H_0^1(U) \). If we consider the Sobolev embedding theorem, it indicates that the functions present in \( H_0^1(U) \) may not sufficiently represent every linear functional possible in the dual space, hence, revealing why \( H^* \) diverges from \( H_0^1(U) \).

This distinction is pivotal in the study of partial differential equations, specifically when applying weak formulations of such equations. Understanding the nature of dual spaces aids in grasping the broader implications of functionals beyond elementary confines of Hilbert spaces—enabling analysis and solutions that are crucial in mathematical analysis and applied studies.
