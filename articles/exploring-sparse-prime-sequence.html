Unearthing the Mysteries of a Sparse Prime Sequence

Recent investigations into a recursively generated prime sequence have unveiled a fascinating area of study within number theory. This sequence, denoted as F, consists of primes that cannot be expressed as a sum of prior primes raised to various powers. It begins with the first prime, 2, and progressively tests subsequent primes for their foundational status, revealing a unique approach to identifying primitive elements in the realm of primes.

The experimental findings extend to the computed terms of this sequence, which now include notable primes like 3, 23, 37, 47, and others, culminating in a total of 15 terms. The sparsity of this sequence, indicated by an erratic gap sequence, raises intriguing questions about its growth and density compared to other known prime sequences. These mathematical dynamics invite deeper exploration beyond merely calculating terms.

Key open questions that arise from this study include:

  • Is this sequence finite, or does it extend infinitely in the prime number landscape?
  • What is the asymptotic growth as we progress through the prime series?
  • How does this sequence relate to known theories regarding the density of primes?

Moreover, the sum of the reciprocals of the first 15 terms approaches 0.95, hinting at significant characteristics that might be bounded below 1. This suggests potential insights into prime distributions and their underlying principles. Continued analysis and engagement from the mathematical community will be vital for understanding the implications of this sparse prime sequence.
