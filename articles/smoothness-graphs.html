The Smoothness Debate: Graphs of Non-Smooth Functions Explained

The discussion surrounding the smoothness of graphs derived from non-smooth functions is fascinating and essential for advanced mathematical studies. In his enlightening work, Lee elaborates on the properties of topological manifolds and how a single chart can satisfactorily define a smooth structure on a manifold. This fundamental principle offers a solid ground for understanding the graph of a continuous function, denoted as $\Gamma(f)$, which represents a manifold characterized by an atlas featuring a single chart.

It is intriguing to note that a continuous function $f:U o\mathbb{R}^m$ can yield $\Gamma(f)$ as a manifold, thereby establishing it as a topological entity. However, when examining smooth manifolds, both Lee and Tu illustrate the necessity of strict conditions—specifically, that $f$ must possess the property of being $C^{\infty}$. This additional criterion significantly enhances the manifold's characteristics, paving the way for a richer understanding of its tangent structures and differentiable functions.

So why do renowned mathematicians insist on emphasizing the smoothness of $f$? The answer lies in the richness of smooth structures. While $\Gamma(f)$ can exist through various non-smooth functions, without the $C^{\infty}$ condition, the manifold lacks differentiable properties crucial for various applications in differential geometry and theoretical physics. Thus, smoothness not only impacts the fundamental properties of $\Gamma(f)$ but also affects its utility in higher-dimensional calculus and beyond.

In conclusion, the inclusion of the smoothness condition around $f$ isn’t merely pedantic; it substantially enriches our understanding of the mathematical landscape. As researchers delve deeper into the essence of manifolds, recognizing this distinction becomes vital for both theoretical exploration and practical application in advanced mathematics.
