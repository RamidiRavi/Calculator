The Intriguing Connection Between Minkowski and Frobenius Products

The study of number theory often intersects with various mathematical constructs, including bilinear forms and inner products. One noteworthy observation arises when comparing the dot product on the Minkowski embedding of a number field and the Frobenius inner product. At the heart of this exploration lies the trace form $tr(ab)$, an intuitive choice for bilinear forms that aligns seamlessly with the familiar dot product in real and complex spaces.

For a number field $K$, the Minkowski embedding serves as a bridge, converting the elements of $K$ into vectors that encapsulate their images across various embeddings. This transformation allows for the natural application of the trace form in calculating discriminants of integral bases, effectively linking algebraic structures to geometric interpretations in Minkowski space.

The assertion that the trace form can be viewed as a Frobenius inner product opens a pathway to further inquiry. By interpreting elements of $K$ as linear transformations $K ightarrow K$, we delve into a broader perspective that mirrors the operations of matrix multiplication. This exploration not only identifies similarities but prompts deeper questions: How do these mathematical structures relate meaningfully to one another, especially within the constraints of number theory?

Furthermore, the discussion of whether the inclusion of a transpose in the Frobenius product alters its application in number fields merits attention. While the conventional Frobenius inner product is defined as $tr(A^TB)$, the implications of this difference could provide critical insights into the overarching geometry and algebra of number fields. If no substantial impact is found, it corroborates the observed coincidences between the bilinear forms, enriching our understanding of their interrelations.
