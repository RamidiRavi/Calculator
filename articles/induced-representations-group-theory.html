Unpacking Induced Representations: Trivial Irrep Insights

Induced representations serve as a pivotal concept in the study of group theory, particularly when analyzing the relationships between a group $G$, and its subgroup $H$. This exploration begins with the trivial irreducible representation of $H$, denoted as $1_{H}$. By inducing this representation from $H$ to $G$, we yield the induced representation $(1_{H})^{G}$, which can be expressed as a direct sum of irreducible representations of $G$.

The decomposition takes the form $(1_{H})^{G} = 1_{G} + \sum_{\chi eq 1} n_{\chi} \chi_{G}$. Here, the intriguing aspect arises in determining the coefficients $n_{\chi}$ for the irreducible representations $\chi_{G}$ that appear in the expansion. A common intuition is that $n_{\chi}$ must be non-zero if $\chi$ restricts to the trivial representation $1_{H}$ on $H$. However, the relationship between $n_{\chi}$ and the dimensions of the conjugacy classes of the coset $G/H$ often requires deeper examination.

Moreover, the inquiry does not solely pertain to finite groups, as these intuitions and structures also translate into the realm of Lie groups, though with additional complexities. In Lie theory, the smoothness and continuity can introduce subtleties not present in the discrete case, warranting additional tools and methods for analysis. For those delving into the determination of subgroup $H$ based on the irreducible representations $\chi_{G}$ observed, the exploration is both rich and intricate. Researchers in the field often employ techniques involving character theory and the exploration of fusion systems to unravel these connections.

To embark further on this mathematical journey, one might look into key literature on representation theory, such as works by Wigner and Serre on characters and representations of finite groups. Participating in forums or engaging with dedicated research groups can also illuminate various nuances, methodologies, and applications of these representations across mathematics.
