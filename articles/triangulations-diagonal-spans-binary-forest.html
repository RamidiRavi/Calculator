Investigating Triangulations of Convex n-gons: A New Insight

Triangulations of convex polygons are a fascinating area of study in computational geometry, with various applications ranging from computer graphics to geographic information systems. A recent lemma concerning these triangulations posits that every triangulation of a convex (n)-gon can be represented as a balanced-root binary forest of diagonal spans. This claim invites deeper investigation into the relationships between triangulations and binary trees, especially through the lenses of internal node weights and diagonal spans.

The lemma states that for any triangulation (T), the sorted list of diagonal spans correlates directly with a binary forest's internal node weights, where each leaf represents a boundary edge. This connection is established by careful selection of triangles and the application of the weighted centroid theorem. The proposed proof seeks to root the triangulation wisely, ensuring that the resultant binary forest accurately reflects the structure of the triangulation.

One of the core elements of this lemma is the choice of a triangle with suitably constrained boundaries, which is shown to guarantee a valid representation. The recursive methodology further enriches the proof, steering the triangulation process within the confines of the complementary arcs. However, the crux lies in validating the internal node weights as direct reflections of the corresponding diagonal spans. Careful scrutiny of this aspect will determine the robustness of the lemma.

As the fields of geometry and graph theory continue to intertwine, unraveling such complexities could lead to exciting new methods for triangulation. For those interested in computational efficiency and theoretical underpinnings, addressing the particularities of this lemma might just yield valuable insights into the overarching structural dynamics of triangulated polygons.
