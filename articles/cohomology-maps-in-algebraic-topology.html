Understanding Cohomology Maps That Commute with Pushforwards

In algebraic topology, understanding the interactions between cohomology groups is critical for bridging concepts from different areas in mathematics. A pivotal aspect of this is the behavior of continuous maps, specifically how they yield pullback operations in cohomology denoted as $f^*$. When considering maps between cohomology groups $H^n(X;G) o H^m(X;A)$, cohomology operations are defined as those that commute with this pullback. However, the intriguing nature of cohomology maps further unfolds when we introduce the concept of pushforwards.

Poincaré duality offers a fascinating avenue for defining pushforward operations in cohomology, represented as $f_!$, which are closely tied to Gysin maps. This operation is expressed via the relationship $f_! = PD^{-1} \circ f_* \circ PD$. The standard pushforward in homology, $f_*: H_*(X) o H_*(Y)$, lays the groundwork for exploring how these pushforwards interact with cohomological structures.

When we consider a map $\alpha: H^n(X;G) o H^m(X;A)$ that commutes with the pushforward $f_!$, the implications become particularly thought-provoking. Such a map can be reinterpreted as a homology operation masked within the cohomological framework. By defining $eta = PD \circ \alpha \circ PD$, one can observe that this new map $eta$ commutes with $f_*$, thereby indicating that the original map $\alpha$ retains meaningful structural properties associated with homology.

Exploring the classification of these operations could lead to fruitful insights. References in the literature are scarce, suggesting that the interplay between these categories, specifically regarding maps that commute with pushforwards, deserves further investigation. To build on this foundation, delving into the mechanisms by which cohomology operations can be connected to spectra, analogous to how they relate to Eilenberg-MacLane spaces, could provide valuable research pathways.
