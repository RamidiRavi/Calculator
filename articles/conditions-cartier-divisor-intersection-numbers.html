Unlocking the Mystery of Cartier Divisor Intersection Numbers

In algebraic geometry, understanding the interplay between Cartier divisors and coherent sheaves is crucial for exploring the depths of geometric properties of varieties. A key question arises when considering if the intersection number of Cartier divisors can equate to the dimension of global sections of a coherent sheaf.

When dealing with a variety $X$ over a field $k$, and a coherent sheaf $F$ with proper support, we can investigate conditions under which the intersection number defined as $int(L_1, ..., L_n, F) = \sum_{S \subseteq \{1, ...,n\}} (-1)^{|S|} \chi(F \otimes (\otimes_{i \in S} L_i^\vee))$ equals the dimension $dim_k(\Gamma(Y, i^* F))$.

For this equality to hold, a vital condition is that the intersection $Y = \cap D_i$ must have dimension zero. Moreover, it's crucial that at each point $y \in Y$, the local defining equations of the Cartier divisors $D_i$ form a regular sequence. This ensures that the local cohomological properties behave consistently, allowing us to connect the global properties expressed through intersection numbers with the structure of global sections of the sheaf $F$.

Furthermore, applying Vakil's insights, if each effective Cartier divisor $L_i = O(D_i)$ avoids associated points of the sheaf $F$, then the relationship between intersection numbers on $D_n$ and $X$ can provide promises for equating the dimensions. This encapsulates a fascinating area of research, offering pathways to enhance our understanding of the interaction between sheaf cohomology and divisor theory.
