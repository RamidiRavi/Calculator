Unlocking Lurie's Proof: Essential Surjectivity Demystified

In the realm of higher topos theory, Proposition 5.5.4.20 presents intriguing challenges and insights. As stated, the proposition establishes a fully faithful functor linking the localization of a presentable $\infty$-category $\mathcal C$ with a target $\infty$-category $D$. Here, the importance of understanding the behavior of colimit-preserving functors cannot be overstated, especially when dealing with small sets of morphisms $S$ that lead to equivalences in $D$.

One critical aspect in proving essential surjectivity is showing that the functor obtained from a colimit-preserving $f:\mathcal C o \mathcal D$ that satisfies $f(s)$ being an equivalence for each morphism in $S$ is, in fact, colimit-preserving. While the proof may not lay out this property directly, it is essential to consider the definitions and properties of localization within category theory.

Localization can often be maneuvered through a limit-preserving lens. Specifically, when one establishes that morphisms in $S^{-1}\mathcal C$ reflect the homotopical properties of their counterparts in $\mathcal C$, it follows that the functor maintains colimit preservation. Essential surjectivity comes into play as one examines how the structural integrity of colimits is preserved under the interaction of these functors, effectively bridging the gap between the localized and original categories.

Engaging deeply with this proof reveals not only the intricacies of higher topological constructs but also the profound implications for understanding equivalences and colimit preservation across various categories. As mathematical research evolves, grappling with these concepts will only enhance the clarity surrounding higher categorical frameworks.
