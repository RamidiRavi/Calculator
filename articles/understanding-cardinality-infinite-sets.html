Mastering Infinite Sets: Proving Cardinality with AC

In the realm of set theory, the concept of cardinality provides a bridge between our understanding of different types of infinity. Specifically, proving that if \( A \) is an infinite set with cardinality \( \kappa \), then \( \kappa = \kappa^2 \) is a fundamental topic. This proof relies on the Axiom of Choice (AC), a principle that, while often controversial, is essential for navigating infinite sets.

A key approach involves constructing a bijection between the set \( A \) and its Cartesian product \( A imes A \). To do this, one can represent elements of the product as pairs, which might initially seem daunting. However, utilizing the AC allows us to pick representatives for each subset and form a pairing strategy. For infinite sets, particularly, this pairing becomes manageable and straightforward.

Start by noting that \( \kappa \) is infinite, which grants substantial flexibility in constructing subsets of \( A \). Specifically, any infinite subset of \( A \) can be used to form a countable collection of pairs—this establishes a correspondence with \( A \) itself. In this manner, you can iteratively create a bijection that retains the cardinality equivalency, ultimately demonstrating \( \kappa = \kappa^2 \).

It’s also insightful to explore various examples. For instance, both \( \mathbb{N} \) and \( \mathbb{R} \) offer concrete illustrations where cardinality aligns with established proofs, leading to the conclusion that these properties extend to all infinite sets with cardinality \( \kappa \). By embracing the intricacies of AC and the structure of infinite sets, mathematicians can confidently assert that \( \kappa = \kappa^2 \), a key feature of the infinite landscape.
