Unlocking the Secrets of Digit Power Inequalities

In the realm of discrete mathematics, inequalities involving powers and digits often reveal unexpected patterns and challenges. The problem at hand concerns proving that for digits \( x \) and \( y \) where \( 1 \leq x \leq 9 \) and \( 0 \leq y \leq 9 \), the inequality holds: $|x^{y}-y^{x}| \le 8^9-9^8$. Furthermore, we aim to identify the specific pairs which satisfy equality.

To tackle this proof, we can leverage the symmetry of the expression. By assuming \( y > x \), we simplify our task, focusing on combinations of digits that maximize the difference \( |x^y - y^x| \). Testing various pairs reveals that boundaries are particularly interesting. For instance, evaluating \( x = 1 \) and \( y = 9 \) gives a small difference of 8. Notably, it’s at the upper boundary \( x = 8 \) and \( y = 9 \) where the maximum occurs, yielding a difference of 91,171,007, computed as \( 8^9 - 9^8 = 134,217,728 - 43,046,721 \).

To prove that no other pairs can achieve a larger difference, we can utilize the growth rates of exponential functions. As \( x \) and \( y \) approach their maximum values within the constraints provided, the resulting values of \( x^y \) and \( y^x \) increase rapidly. The pair \( (8, 9) \) maximizes the difference due to the fact that the base is larger and the exponent is slightly increasing, whereas \( y^x \) has a smaller base and a higher exponent, thus resulting in a smaller outcome relative to the first. Thus, achieving a maximal difference at these endpoint values becomes evident.

In conclusion, the pair \( (8, 9) \) indeed maximizes the digit power difference, aligning with the upper bound proposed in the inequality. The significance of this result transcends mere numeric boundaries, highlighting the intriguing interplay between elementary operations and exponential growth within a discrete set of numbers.
