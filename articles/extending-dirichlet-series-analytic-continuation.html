Analytic Continuation of Dirichlet Series: Possibilities & Examples

The study of Dirichlet series is a cornerstone of analytic number theory, particularly in the context of extending functions beyond their initial domain. As noted, certain Dirichlet series can indeed be analytically continued from the region where the real part of \(s\) is greater than 1 to regions where the real part is non-negative. This is primarily achieved through clever relationships with the Riemann zeta function as outlined in equations (2) and (3) in the original query.

The relationships involving sums \(b(n)\) and \(c(n)\) are of paramount importance in understanding how well a specific Dirichlet series can be generalized. The prime zeta function, \(P(s)\), provides an exemplary model. Its analytical continuation showcases how these relationships can be employed creatively to expand the realm of Dirichlet series, broadening their applicability and insights into prime distributions.

A key factor in determining whether such extensions are feasible lies in the decay rates of the sequences \(b(n)\) and \(c(n)\). For analytic continuation to work effectively and converge in the region \(Re(s) \geq 0\), we require that both \(b(n)\) and \(c(n)\) decrease sufficiently rapidly, ideally faster than any polynomial growth. This condition is critical since slower rates could lead to divergence or undefined behavior in these extended series.

In terms of noteworthy examples beyond the prime zeta function, further explorations can include generalized Dirichlet series where specific \(a(n)\) follow particular forms. Identifying suitable \(b(n)\) and \(c(n)\) sequences with adequate decay could unveil a wealth of new series and deepen our understanding of number theory. Ongoing research is needed to ascertain detailed decay conditions, which will ultimately help in successfully extending these Dirichlet series across broader domains.
