Are Regular Conditional Distributions Pushforward Measures?

The world of probability and measure theory often leads mathematicians into complex and intricate discussions. One such discussion revolves around the concept of regular conditional distributions and their potential characterization as pushforward measures of regular conditional probabilities. To explore this concept, we begin with a probability space $(\Omega, \mathcal{A}, P)$, where $\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{A}$ serves as a sub-sigma-algebra and $X:(\Omega, \mathcal{A}) ightarrow (\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{F})$ represents a random variable.

A regular conditional distribution, denoted $P_{X}(\cdot|\mathcal{G})$, manifests under two primary conditions: first, it is a version of $P(X\in F|\mathcal{G})$ for all measurable sets $F\in\mathcal{F}$; and second, for each individual $\omega\in\Omega$, $P_X(\cdot|\mathcal{G})(\omega)$ maintains its status as a probability measure. The intriguing part is the supposition that the first condition suggests a connection to the notion of pushforward measures.
