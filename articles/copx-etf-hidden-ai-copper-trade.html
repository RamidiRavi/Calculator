Forget Software: Why the COPX ETF Is the Hidden AI Pick-and-Shovel Trade

When people think about investing in Artificial Intelligence (AI), software and semiconductor stocks often come to mind. But there's another critical component driving AI that is often overlooked — copper. This essential metal powers everything from data centers to GPU racks, making copper miners a crucial part of the AI supply chain. The Global X Copper Miners ETF (NYSE:COPX) offers investors a direct way to capitalize on this trend with a diversified portfolio of copper mining companies.

What Makes COPX Stand Out?

COPX tracks the Solactive Global Copper Miners index, concentrating on multiple established copper producers globally. Unlike typical tech-focused AI plays, COPX taps into the physical infrastructure backbone of AI — the copper that transmits electricity across data centers and other electrified infrastructure.

  • Substantial Returns: COPX has delivered an impressive 69% return over the past year, significantly outpacing the broader S&P 500's 20% during the same period.
  • Diverse Holdings: The ETF includes large producers like Freeport-McMoRan and Southern Copper, which have shown strong year-over-year growth.
  • Growth Catalyst: Increasing global copper demand fueled by AI data centers and electrification innovations.

The Role of Copper in AI and Electrification

Every piece of AI infrastructure — from substations to high-capacity cables feeding GPUs — depends heavily on copper. According to the National Electrical Contractors Association, data center energy use alone could constitute 9.1% of all U.S. electricity consumption by 2030. As AI computing needs grow, so does copper consumption, projected to rise from 26 million tonnes in 2022 to 43 million tonnes by 2050.

Understanding Copper's Critical Usage

  • Data Centers: Thousands of tons of copper wire are used to move power efficiently to and within data centers.
  • Electrification: Copper is vital for electric vehicles (EVs) and renewable energy systems, both connected to AI advancements.
  • Infrastructure: From substations to transformers to high-voltage cables, copper is the backbone of electric power transmission.

Risks and Tradeoffs

Like any investment, COPX comes with certain risks:

  1. Cyclicality: Copper miner stocks are sensitive to global economic cycles, often amplifying market swings. For example, COPX experienced nearly a 3% drop in a recent month reflecting growth concerns.
  2. Geopolitical Exposure: A significant portion of copper comes from countries like Chile, Peru, and the Democratic Republic of Congo, involving political and regulatory risks.
  3. Volatility: COPX can face sharp price fluctuations, making it less suitable for investors who need stable income or low volatility.
  4. Technological Shifts: Emerging fiber optics and silicon photonics could alter some copper applications in the future but are unlikely to replace copper's role in power transmission.

Who Should Consider Investing in COPX?

COPX is ideal for:

  • Investors seeking to diversify beyond software and semiconductors into the physical infrastructure fueling AI.
  • Those willing to accept cyclical ups and downs for potentially higher long-term gains.
  • Investors looking for targeted exposure to electrification trends without individual stock picking risks.

Who should avoid it? Retirees or conservative investors needing stable income or low volatility should be cautious due to the fund’s high fluctuation and minimal dividend payouts.

How to Incorporate COPX into a Balanced Portfolio

As a satellite position complementing broad equity index funds, COPX can balance a portfolio exposed to AI growth:

  • Pair COPX with tech-heavy investments such as software or semiconductor ETFs to cover both physical and digital AI components.
  • Consider allocating 5-10% of your portfolio to COPX, depending on your risk tolerance.
  • Use diversified instruments like COPX to reduce company-specific risk inherent in mining stocks.
