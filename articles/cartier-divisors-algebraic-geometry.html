Mastering Cartier Divisors: A Step-by-Step Guide

The exploration of Cartier divisors is a fundamental aspect of algebraic geometry, particularly when studying varieties defined by explicit equations. This article delves into a specific problem involving the computation of Cartier divisors from Weil divisors, shedding light on the concepts and common pitfalls.

We start with a variety \(X\) defined as \( ext{Spec} k[x, y, z, w]/(xy - zw) \) embedded in \(A^4_k\). In part (a) of the problem, we establish that \(D = V(x, z)\) is indeed a prime (Weil) divisor and that the class group \( ext{Cl}(X) \) is isomorphic to \( \mathbb{Z} \), generated by the divisor class of \(D\). This step is straightforward for those familiar with the basic definitions and properties of Weil divisors.

Part (b) introduces more complexity. We take \(Y = X \setminus \{ O \}\), where \(O\) is the origin in \(A^4_k\). The goal is to show that the intersection \(D_Y = D \cap Y\) defines a Cartier divisor on \(Y\). An open cover of \(Y\) can be constructed using the sets \(D(x), D(y), D(z), D(w)\). The challenge arises when determining the appropriate transition maps on this cover.

While analyzing the ideals in local rings, one observes that on \(D(y)\) the function \(x = rac{zw}{y}\) transforms the ideal \( (z) \) into a simpler form. A common mistake occurs when attempting to verify the unit condition on overlaps, particularly on the intersection \(D(xy)\) where \(z\) may not behave as expected. It turns out that a deeper understanding of the structure of intersection localizations is required to confirm the definitions of Cartier divisors. Recognizing these subtleties is crucial for students and researchers engaging with similar topics.
