Geometric Insights into the Annihilator of Zero Divisors

The study of zero divisors in a reduced Noetherian ring offers rich geometric interpretations, particularly through the lens of irreducible components of the spectrum of the ring, $\mathrm{Spec} R$. A significant relationship arises when we look at an element $f \in R$ that serves as a zero divisor. By definition, $f$ is a zero divisor precisely when the vanishing set $V(f)$ contains an irreducible component of $\mathrm{Spec} R$.

To delve deeper, the exploration of the annihilator of $f$ provides substantial insights. Specifically, one may ask whether the annihilator can be determined through the irreducible components associated with $f$. Taking the example of the ring $R = k[x, y]/(xy)$, we observe that the annihilator of $x$ is $y$, which intriguingly corresponds to the other irreducible component of the space, namely $V(y)$.

This leads to a captivating proposition: if $V(f)$ encompasses an irreducible component such as $V(x)$, could we conclude that the annihilator $\mathrm{Ann}(f)$ is captured by the generators relating to the other components? This hypothesis finds validity in certain contexts, where one can indeed express $\mathrm{Ann}(f)$ as the ideal generated by the elements corresponding to the remaining irreducible components.

In summation, unraveling the connection between zero divisors and their annihilators through the geometric view not only enhances our algebraic understanding but also invites further exploration of how these concepts interact across various ring structures. Such insights not only deepen the theoretical framework but also open avenues for practical applications in algebraic geometry and commutative algebra.
