Exploring Stopped Local Martingales and Their Martingale Properties

In stochastic processes, especially in the realm of financial mathematics, understanding the properties of martingales is crucial. A local martingale is a generalization of a martingale that allows for the inclusion of processes that may not be martingales in the classical sense. Here, we focus on a specific case involving a continuous local martingale defined as $M$.

Let’s consider a local martingale $M$ with initial value $M_0 = 0$ and an arbitrary small real number $\epsilon > 0$. We introduce the stopping time $ au = \inf \{ t \ge 0 : |M_t| > \epsilon \}$ and define the process $N:= M^2 - [M]$. The objective is to show that the process $N^ au$ forms a martingale.

One key aspect in proving that $N^ au$ is a martingale is demonstrating its boundedness. Since $M$ is continuous, at time $ au$, the absolute value of the martingale is constrained by the defined limit $\epsilon$, which means that $|M_{ au}| \leq \epsilon$. Therefore, for any time $t$, we have $|M_{t \wedge au}| \leq \epsilon$, leading to the conclusion that $M_{t \wedge au}^2 \leq \epsilon^2$. But it raises an important question: why is the quadratic variation $[M]$ also bounded in this scenario?
