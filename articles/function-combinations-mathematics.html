Is It Possible to Combine Filter Functions? A Mathematical Inquiry

The question posed is a fascinating exploration into the realm of mathematical analysis, particularly relating to integrable functions. The core of this inquiry revolves around whether it's possible to define a third function, $F(\omega)$, such that its exponential representation can be expressed as a combination of two other functions, $F_1(\omega)$ and $F_2(\omega)$, weighted by a third arbitrary function $S(\omega)$.

The fundamental property of $S(\omega)$ being a normalized function, where the integral equals one, allows it to act as a kind of weighting mechanism. However, it is crucial to recognize that the expression:

$e^{-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}S(\omega)F(\omega)d\omega} = e^{-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}S(\omega)F_1(\omega)d\omega} + e^{-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}S(\omega)F_2(\omega)d\omega}$

is not simply a linear combination of $F_1(\omega)$ and $F_2(\omega)$. The exponentials of integrals suggest a more complex interaction which yields constraints that likely cannot be satisfied by constructing $F(\omega)$ as a straightforward linear combination of its predecessors.

In conclusion, while the hypothesis seems plausible, the mathematical implications suggest that no such function $F(\omega)$ can exist that would fulfill this requirement for any arbitrary weighting function $S(\omega)$. A formal proof could delve into properties of convex functions and the nature of exponential mappings to further solidify this assertion. For those inclined towards mathematical proof and theory, this inquiry opens the door to deeper investigations into function behavior and the interplay of integrable forms.
